FFF Privacy Policy

Effective date: 25 July 2026

FFF is a game about claiming real ground. To draw your route and work out which hexagons you crossed, the app has to know where you are. That is a serious thing to ask for, so this page says exactly what is recorded, who can see it, where it is kept and how to erase it. Plain words, no legal fog.

The short version

  • There is no sign up. No email, no password, no phone number.
  • Your location is recorded only while an activity is running. You start it, you stop it.
  • Other players see your display name, your handle, your colour and the hexes you hold. They never see your route line, your start point or where you are right now.
  • Nothing is sold. No ads, no ad networks, no tracking SDKs, no third party analytics.
  • You can erase everything, on your phone and on the server, from Settings in two taps.

Who is responsible

FFF is built and run by one independent developer, Safak Gungor, based in Istanbul, Türkiye. That person is the data controller. Write to fff.turf.app@gmail.com about anything on this page and a human will answer.

What FFF stores

This is the whole list. Nothing outside it is collected.

  • Precise GPS coordinates, but only during an activity. Between GO and Finish the app records latitude, longitude, a timestamp and a speed reading a few times a minute. This is what draws your route line, measures your distance and decides which hexes you crossed.
  • Derived hex cell ids. Each recorded point is turned into an H3 resolution 9 cell id, an area of roughly 0.1 square kilometres. These ids are the game itself. They live on the server so rivals can see who owns what.
  • Your display name and your handle. You choose both when you first open the app. They are visible to strangers, so pick something you are happy for strangers to read.
  • Vehicle details you type into the garage. Type, year, make, model and an optional nickname. Free text, typed by you, used to tag activities and to print a line on a share card.
  • An anonymous per install device id. A random string your phone generates the first time FFF opens. It is how the server knows which territory is yours without knowing who you are. It is not your Apple ID, not your advertising id and not your phone's serial number. Delete and reinstall the app and you become a brand new player with no history.
  • An Expo push token, only if you turn notifications on. This is the address your phone gives us so a steal alert can reach it.
  • Activity summaries and game state. Distance, duration, average and top speed, hexes claimed, hexes stolen, XP, level, streak, badges, personal bests and your weekly totals.
  • Reports you send about another player. The handle you reported, the reason, your optional note, your device id and the time. See the Community Rules for what happens next.

Two more things worth being clear about. While you are recording, the points stay on your phone. They are sent to the server when you tap Finish. And when the map opens, the app sends your current position to the server so it can send back the territory around you. That position is used to answer the question and is not kept as a location history.

What FFF does not collect

  • No email address, no password, no phone number, no date of birth.
  • No contacts, no calendar, no microphone, no camera, no photo library. Your share card is drawn on your phone and handed straight to the iOS share sheet.
  • No advertising identifier and no IDFA. FFF does not track you across other apps or websites, which is why you never see the App Tracking Transparency prompt.
  • No third party analytics, no crash tracking SDK, no attribution SDK, no ad networks.
  • No background location. When no activity is running, FFF is not watching where you go.
  • No selling, renting or trading of your data. Not now, not later. If that ever changed it would need a new policy and a new agreement from you, and FFF has no plans to change it.

What other players can see

FFF is one shared map, so some of your data is meant to be seen. Other players near you can see your handle, your display name, your colour, the hexes you currently hold, your weekly distance and steal totals, and your place on the area leaderboard. When you take a hex from someone, they get a notification naming your handle. That is the game.

Other players cannot see your route line, your individual GPS points, your address, your start or end point, your live position, your garage or your activity history.

Ghost Mode, in Settings, hides your handle, your colour and your territory from other players' maps and from the nearby list while it is on. Your own recording carries on as normal and your hexes still count for you.

Where your data lives

  • The database is Supabase, a hosted Postgres service. The FFF project runs in Supabase's European Union region in Frankfurt, Germany. Supabase acts as our processor and does not use your data for its own purposes.
  • Push notifications go through Expo's push service, run by 650 Industries, which hands the message to Apple's Push Notification service for delivery. Both see the push token and the text of the alert, which is why alerts say things like "someone stole 2 of your hexes" and never anything private.
  • Apple runs the App Store. If you subscribe, Apple handles the payment and tells us only whether a subscription is active. Your card details never touch FFF and are never visible to us.
  • Subscription state is checked through RevenueCat, which receives an anonymous subscriber id and the receipt Apple issues. It never receives your location, your handle or your territory.
  • The developer is based in Türkiye and reaches the database from there. Expo and Apple may process the push token outside the European Economic Area. Those transfers rely on the standard contractual clauses those companies publish.

How long it is kept

  • The GPS coordinates of a finished activity are kept for 12 months, then deleted automatically. The activity summary and the hexes it earned you survive that deletion.
  • Hex ownership follows the game clock. A hex lives 7 days, plus 3 days for each of up to 3 reinforcements. When it expires it rots into a grey ruin owned by The Rust and anyone can reclaim it. Expired ownership rows are cleared 30 days later.
  • Your profile, handle, garage, totals and badges stay for as long as you keep playing, and are deleted 18 months after your last activity if you never come back.
  • Your push token is deleted as soon as you turn notifications off, or when Apple tells us the token is dead.
  • Reports and the notes attached to them are kept for 12 months so repeat offenders can be spotted.

Deleting everything

Open Settings and tap "Delete my account and data". One confirmation, no email, no waiting. It removes your profile, your handle, your display name, your garage, every activity and every GPS point, your push token and your device id from the server, and wipes the copy held on your phone. The hexes you held are released and become grey ruins for someone else to take.

There is no login, so there is no way to bring any of it back. Deleting the app from your phone on its own does not delete the server side, so use the button first if you want a clean exit.

If the app will not open, email fff.turf.app@gmail.com from any address with the device id shown in Settings under About, and it will be deleted for you within 30 days.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you have the right to ask for a copy of your data, to correct it, to have it deleted, to take it elsewhere in a readable file, and to object to how it is used. Because FFF holds no email address and no account, requests are matched to the device id in Settings under About. Send it to fff.turf.app@gmail.com and you will get an answer within 30 days. If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom you can also complain to your local data protection authority. If you are in Türkiye you can apply to the KVKK.

Children

FFF is not built for children. Do not use it if you are under 13, or under the age of digital consent where you live, which is 16 in some European countries, unless a parent or guardian agrees on your behalf. We do not knowingly keep data from children under those ages. If you believe a child is using FFF, email fff.turf.app@gmail.com and the account will be deleted.

Names other people can see

Handles and display names are written by players and shown to strangers, so they are filtered when they are created and can be reported by anyone. Reports are reviewed within 24 hours. Names that break the rules are removed and repeat offenders lose access to the shared map. The full rules live on the Community Rules page in the app.

Security

The app talks to the database over HTTPS. Writes go through restricted server functions rather than open access to the tables, and the key shipped inside the app cannot read or change another player's rows. No system is perfect, and if a breach ever affects your data you will be told in the app and the relevant authority will be notified.

A word about moving

Nothing in FFF rewards speed. Territory, distance, streaks and comebacks are the whole scoreboard. Start your activity before you set off, put the phone in a mount or a pocket, and leave it alone until you stop. Obey the road. No hex is worth it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version appears here with a new effective date, and anything that meaningfully changes what is collected or who sees it will be shown in the app before it takes effect.
